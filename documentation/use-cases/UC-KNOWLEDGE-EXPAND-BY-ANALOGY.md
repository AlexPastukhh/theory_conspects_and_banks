# UC-KNOWLEDGE-EXPAND-BY-ANALOGY — Expand Through Analogy and Comparison

## Situation
Area A is already known, while an analogous or potentially analogous Area B is unknown or weaker.
The owner wants to use A as an anchor without assuming a direct equivalent exists.

## Result
An explicit comparative model that explains:
- what genuinely transfers from the known side;
- what is only partially analogous;
- where the same problem is solved differently;
- where similar surface hides a different underlying model;
- important non-equivalences and analogy limits;
- newly exposed assumptions in the previously known Area.

When the comparison itself is durable knowledge, it may be integrated as a comparison Knowledge Unit owned by the broader Concept that gives the comparison meaning.

## Process
1. Define the underlying engineering/domain responsibility first; named tools/frameworks are manifestations, not the comparison axis by default.
2. Define known side A and target side B only where both are relevant manifestations/solutions.
3. Select the comparison axis and allow `NO_USEFUL_DIRECT_EQUIVALENT` / `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
4. State the current model of A.
5. When useful, predict B before research.
6. Research B without forcing equivalence.
7. Describe in ordinary language what transfers, what is only partially analogous, what differs, and where the analogy breaks.
8. Explain important differences and their causes.
9. Revisit A in light of the comparison and explicitly check whether B exposed a previously unnoticed gap in A or in the generic responsibility.
10. Integrate durable comparative knowledge, preferably as a comparison Knowledge Unit when the comparison itself is worth retaining.
11. Route new gaps/questions into Coverage & Expansion / expansion planning.

## Boundary
Finding that there is **no useful direct equivalent** is a valid successful outcome.

The Use Case does not require a machine-readable pairwise analogy relation, fixed relation enum, relation graph, or one-to-one framework/tool counterpart. Analogy is a secondary completeness/transfer technique after the underlying responsibility is understood.
